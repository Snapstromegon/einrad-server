# Unicycle jumps

IUF Chapter 13 defines four events: High Jump over Bar, High Jump onto
Platform, Long Jump on Track, and Long Jump on Platform. The common result
principle is to record the best successful attempt; the event-specific
landing/control requirements determine success. (IUF 2026, §§13A.1,
13B.5, pp.226-227.)

## Safety and attempts

- Long Jump on Track: shoes, helmet, knee pads, and gloves.
- Long Jump on Platform: shoes, helmet, and knee pads.
- High Jump over Bar and High Jump onto Platform: shoes and helmet; shin
  guards are strongly recommended.
- A regular unicycle is required, with no wheel or crank-size restrictions.
- Riders get at most three attempts at any one distance in preliminaries and
  finals, and at most 12 total preliminary attempts. Riders may skip distances,
  but once they attempt a distance they must complete it before moving higher;
  attempts cannot go back down.
(IUF 2026, §§13B.1-13B.7, pp.227-228.)

## What makes a successful attempt

- **High Jump over Bar:** mount before the start line; clear the bar without
  knocking it down; land and stay in control across the finish without
  dismounting or touching the ground / apparatus. The recorded result is the
  highest completed bar height.
- **High Jump onto Platform:** land with the tire only on the landing surface
  (no pedal or crank grabs) and stay mounted there for a judge's three-second
  count.
- **Long Jump on Track:** clear the jump marker and landing marker, then ride
  in control over the finish line. Touching a marker is a foul. Distance is
  measured between the outer edges of the jump and landing markers.
- **Long Jump on Platform:** start stationary on the take-off platform, land
  on the landing platform without touching the ground, and remain mounted and
  in control for three seconds.
(IUF 2026, §§13B.8-13B.11, pp.228-230.)

## Officials, facilities, and ties

The host designates a Jump Director, Chief Judge, and judge. National
championships and Unicons need two judges for Long Jump, one at each marker.
Required space includes 15 m runways and 3 m landing areas for High Jump, and
a 25 m runway with an 8 m landing area for Long Jump on Track. Equal successful
distances are resolved by fewer attempts at the last cleared distance, then
fewer total failures; unresolved ties are shared except for first place, where
a jump-off applies unless the governing event rules say otherwise. (IUF 2026,
§§13C-13D.1-13D.4, 13D.9, pp.231-234.)
