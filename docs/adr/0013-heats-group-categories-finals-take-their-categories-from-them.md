# Heats group categories, finals take their categories from them

A heat is its own entity (#338): a unique name, an optional description and its categories, which share one act type and, for singles, gender. A category belongs to at most one heat (`categories.heat`), and group categories belong to none, as they are their own final. A final's slot group names the heat it is filled from (`final_slots.heat`), the slot is called after it ("1. Finalist (E EM 1)"), and a final's categories are no longer kept by hand but are those of its heats. Every heat of a final shares the final's discipline, and only singles and pairs have finals. The timeplan stays per category: a heat runs as its categories' blocks, and the timeplan view warns when they land on different days. Before, which categories fed a slot group lived only in the free-text slot label ("U17–U21"), so neither placing acts nor the coming result import could tell which acts compete for which slots.

The migration builds the heats from the existing finals once. Each slot group becomes a heat named after its final, with 1, 2, … added when the final has several groups ("E EM 1", "E EM 2"), and without a description. A final with one slot group gives its heat all its categories of the final's discipline. Otherwise the label is read as an age class: "U17–U21" (or with a hyphen) takes the final's categories with an `under_age` from 17 to 21, "U23+" those from 23 on and those without an upper age, and "U11" only U11. A label read any other way leaves its heat empty, and the admin UI marks it for the admin to fill. On an overlap the first match wins, by final order and then start order. The real competition files and the demo all match fully.

Competition files are not converted: a file whose finals still have slot `label`s or their own `categories` is rejected with a message that names the final and shows a short `[[heats]]` and `heat = "…"` example. Unlike the age classes (ADR 0012), turning a label into categories is a guess, and a guess that is silently wrong would only show on the event day, when acts are placed. The guessing therefore lives only in the migration, where it runs once on data the organizer reviews right afterwards. Older files have to be rewritten by hand.

ADR 0014 builds on this: a heat fills at most one final, and its preliminary round (Vorlauf) can be skipped so its acts start directly in that final. In the UI a heat is now called "Vorlaufklasse"; "Vorlauf" means its preliminary round.

ADR 0015 amends this: group categories get heats and finals too, so "group categories belong to none" and "only singles and pairs have finals" no longer hold.
